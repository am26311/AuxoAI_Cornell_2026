# Write your customer persona

> **You vs your customer**
> **You** are a business analyst at Dell. You design the study, build the agent, judge the evidence, and recommend.
> **Your customer** is the person your agent pretends to be while it shops dell.com and a competitor. You write them; the agent plays them.

## Why an AI persona, and its limits

- **Why:** it's a fast, cheap way to walk a website as a specific kind of customer before paying for real research. You can try many customer types in an afternoon.
- **How it differs from a "real" persona:** a marketing persona describes a real customer group, built from interviews and data. An AI persona is a *simulated* customer acting from your short description. It has no real budget, memories or feelings.
- **Limits:**
  - It can misread a page or click the wrong thing. That's an *agent error*, not a Dell problem.
  - It's often more patient and consistent than real people.
  - It can't tell you real sales.
- **So every finding is a hypothesis** to test with real customers. Don't post findings publicly.

## How to use this file

1. Copy the 5-field template below into a new file called **`our-persona.md`** in this folder.
2. Write your own customer, or start from one of the examples and change it. Make it specific.
3. Each of you writes your own for the practice run and the build. For Dell and the competitor, your team picks one persona and everyone uses it, so the comparison is fair.

**Fixed for everyone:** a new laptop, Dell vs your competitor, the tasks Find → Evaluate → Decide, and New York ZIP 10001.

## The four questions your customer answers

After each site, your customer answers four questions. Each one is a **stage of Dell's sales funnel**, so you read the answers as Dell's analyst: *where in the funnel would we lose this customer, and why?*

| Funnel stage | What your customer is asked | In plain words | Why Dell cares |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | Did you find a product that fits your needs and budget? Which one, and at what price? | Could they find something that fits? Answered as yes, partly or no. | Can customers find the right product, or do we lose them in search and navigation? |
| **Product page** | How confident are you that it meets your must-haves? What was missing or unclear? | Did the page answer their questions? Answered as a number from 1 to 5. | Does the page answer their questions, or do they leave to compare elsewhere? |
| **Price transparency** | Do you know the total cost, including tax and delivery, and the return terms? What couldn't you find? | Were the real costs and return terms clear? Answered as yes, partly or no. | Could hidden costs or unclear returns cause cart abandonment? |
| **Purchase intent** | How likely are you to buy here rather than leave? What one thing would change your mind? | Would they buy here, and what is holding them back? Answered as a number from 1 to 5. | Overall, where does this site win or lose against the competitor? |

**Optional:** add one question of your own as field 6 (for example, Marcus: "Is the screen's color accuracy stated?").
These are a synthetic customer's answers: they suggest *where* customers might drop off, not *how many*.

---

## Template (5 fields + 1 optional)

```
1. Who they are: (name, age, job, lives in New York)
2. What they need the laptop for: (one or two sentences)
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $
4. What matters most: (2–3 must-haves)
5. What makes them leave: (deal-breakers, and how patient they are)
6. Our extra question (optional): (one question you want answered after each site)
```

## Strong vs weak

| Field | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Need | "General use" | "Excel models with 200k rows and Power BI, on trains between client sites" |
| What matters most | "Good specs" | "16 GB memory, under 3.5 lb, 10+ hours battery" |
| What makes them leave | "Bad websites" | "Delivery cost only shown at checkout. Gives up after two confusing pages" |

**Rule of thumb:** if two very different people could both match your persona, make it more specific.

## Five examples

*Budgets are drafts and get checked against current dell.com prices before the event.*

### Jordan: everyday / first job
```
1. Who they are: Jordan Lee, 23, operations coordinator starting a first full-time job, lives in New York.
2. What they need the laptop for: Spreadsheets, video calls and email, ready before the job starts in two weeks.
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $1,500
4. What matters most: Comfortable keyboard, decent webcam, all-day battery.
5. What makes them leave: Spec jargon with no explanation, prices that jump when options are added, unclear returns. Average patience.
```

### Priya: business
```
1. Who they are: Priya Shah, 29, business analyst at a consulting firm, lives in New York and travels weekly.
2. What they need the laptop for: Large Excel models, Power BI dashboards and Teams calls on the move.
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $1,300
4. What matters most: At least 16 GB memory, under 3.5 lb, 10+ hours battery.
5. What makes them leave: Battery claims with no detail, hidden memory size, pushy add-ons. Patient, reads the spec sheet.
```

### Marcus: creative
```
1. Who they are: Marcus Reed, 34, freelance video editor, lives in New York.
2. What they need the laptop for: 4K video editing and color grading for client work.
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $2,000
4. What matters most: Dedicated graphics card, 32 GB memory, color-accurate screen.
5. What makes them leave: Vague "creator" labels with no specs, missing screen details, long delivery times. Very patient with specs, impatient with marketing.
6. Our extra question (optional): Is the screen's color accuracy stated?
```

### Dev: developer
```
1. Who they are: Dev Patel, 27, software developer at a startup, lives in New York.
2. What they need the laptop for: Coding, running containers and local databases, sometimes Linux.
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $1,600
4. What matters most: 32 GB memory (or upgradable), fast storage, a good keyboard.
5. What makes them leave: "Up to" claims, missing port lists, bundled software pushed on them. Skeptical, medium patience.
```

### Chris: gaming parent
```
1. Who they are: Chris Morales, 41, logistics manager and parent of two, lives in New York.
2. What they need the laptop for: Weekend gaming, plus the kids' homework during the week.
3. Budget, including tax and delivery: $1,200
4. What matters most: A dedicated graphics card, sturdy build, a warranty that covers accidents.
5. What makes them leave: Warranty terms hidden behind links, confusing model names. Low patience; leaves after two confusing pages.
```
